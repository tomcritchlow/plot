# Notes

## Working Handle

Primary handle: **Marketing's Three-Body Problem**

Core metaphor:

In physics, the three-body problem describes the difficulty of predicting the motion of three gravitational bodies interacting with each other. For marketing, the three bodies are channels, customers, and the company. AI is changing all three at once, so the system becomes unstable if you treat any one body as fixed.

Opening anecdote:

King Oscar II's prize competition asked for progress on a celestial mechanics problem around system stability. Poincare won, then an error was found, and the corrected work helped open the door to chaos theory. Useful line: the story is not a genius proving stability; it is someone looking for stability and discovering that the motion itself was stranger than expected.

Management theory spine:

- Karl Weick / sensemaking: in uncertainty, leaders help groups turn ambiguous circumstances into a shared situation that supports action.
- Teece / dynamic capabilities: firms win in changing environments by integrating, building, and reconfiguring capabilities, not by defending a static plan.
- Railroads / standard time: transformative technologies force new coordination layers. Railroads needed standardized time; AI marketing needs shared standards for evidence, claims, model usage, review, measurement, and memory.

Alternate handles:

- **The Three-Plane Shift**: useful as a supporting explanation, but less memorable.
- **The AI Marketing Triangle**: clearer, but more generic.
- **The Three AI Surfaces**: softer and more interface-oriented.
- **The Three Fronts of AI Marketing**: punchier, but a little too warlike.
- **Channels, Customers, Capabilities**: useful as a subtitle or diagnostic shorthand.
- **Visibility, Interpretation, Capability**: stronger for the consulting/diagnostic version.

## Core Thesis

AI disruption in marketing is not one thing. It is three simultaneous shifts:

1. Channels are changing: search, social, feeds, paid/owned/earned, citations, summaries, synthetic media, authenticity signals.
2. Customers are changing: people use AI to search, shop, compare, interpret, summarize, and delegate parts of the journey.
3. Ways of working are changing: marketing teams use AI internally, which changes production, review, taste, governance, organizational learning, and the speed/quality tradeoff.

The important move is not naming three buckets. The important move is insisting that marketers must manage the interaction between the bodies.

## Useful Diagnostic Language

- Channel body: visibility, findability, distribution, trust, citation, synthesis.
- Customer body: interpretation, delegated decision-making, expectation, cognitive infrastructure.
- Company body: capability, governance, judgment, quality, workflows, brand memory.

Possible framework question:

> Can the market find you? Can the model understand you? Can the organization learn fast enough to keep up?

## Tension

AI makes it easier for marketers to produce more marketing at exactly the moment customers and channels are becoming less tolerant of generic synthetic content.

This makes coherence more valuable than volume.

## So-What / Provocations

1. Stop treating AI as a channel problem.
2. Map the pull between channels, customers, and the company.
3. Build for interpretation, not just attention.
4. Make the customer journey machine-readable.
5. Treat slop as an operating risk.
6. Separate speed from learning.
7. Create new coordination layers.
8. Look for coherence, not coverage.
9. Give the CMO a dashboard for visibility, interpretation, and capability.
10. Lead like the system is moving.

## Possible Follow-up Artifacts

- 2x2 or 3-column diagnostic.
- Workshop outline: Channel readiness, Customer readiness, Operating readiness.
- CMO briefing slide: "AI does not disrupt marketing in one place."
- Visual: three gravitational bodies labeled Channels, Customers, Company, with distorted orbit lines around "brand preference" or "growth."
- Visual: an orbital map where the old marketing funnel is bent by three new gravitational pulls.
- Visual: a simple triangle, but with each node rendered as a planet/body and arrows showing feedback loops rather than static pillars.
- Maturity model:
  - Reactive: treating AI as SEO/tooling.
  - Fragmented: teams experimenting independently.
  - Aligned: channel/customer/company bodies mapped together.
  - Compounding: organization has shared evidence, evaluation, and learning loops.
