# Sources

- [Institut Mittag-Leffler: Prize Competition](https://www.mittag-leffler.se/about-us/history/prize-competition/)
  - Why it matters: Primary historical context for King Oscar II's prize competition and Poincare's role in the three-body problem story.

- [Open Research Online: June Barrow-Green, Poincare and the Three Body Problem](https://oro.open.ac.uk/57403/)
  - Why it matters: Historical source for Poincare's memoir and its role in early mathematical descriptions of chaotic behavior.

- [Organization Science: Organizing and the Process of Sensemaking](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/orsc.1050.0133)
  - Why it matters: Management-theory grounding for frameworks as shared sensemaking tools in uncertain environments.

- [Harvard Business School: Dynamic Capabilities and Strategic Management](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=3256)
  - Why it matters: Management-theory grounding for the idea that firms need to integrate, build, and reconfigure capabilities in changing environments.

- [Ronald Coase: The Nature of the Firm](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1468-0335.1937.tb00002.x)
  - Why it matters: Economic grounding for firms as coordination structures shaped by transaction costs.

- [Smithsonian National Museum of American History: Time Zones](https://americanhistory.si.edu/ontime/synchronizing/zones.html)
  - Why it matters: Historical analogy for technological revolutions creating new coordination layers. Railroads helped standardize local times into time zones on November 18, 1883.

- [Google: Search I/O 2026 updates](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/search-io-2026/)
  - Why it matters: Primary source for AI Mode growth, more conversational search, and agentic search features.

- [BuzzStream: Which News Sites Block AI Crawlers in 2025?](https://www.buzzstream.com/blog/publishers-block-ai-study/)
  - Why it matters: Data source for the 79% AI training bot blocking and 71% live retrieval bot blocking figures.

- [Pew: Americans and AI 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/americans-and-ai-2026-chatbots-smart-devices-and-views-on-impact/)
  - Why it matters: Current consumer behavior data. Pew reports that 42% of U.S. adults use chatbots to search for information and 38% of employed adults use them for work tasks.

- [Pew: Google users are less likely to click links when an AI summary appears](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/)
  - Why it matters: Useful evidence for the channel body. AI summaries change click behavior, not just search-page aesthetics.

- [Adobe: Generative AI-powered shopping rises with traffic to U.S. retail sites](https://business.adobe.com/blog/generative-ai-powered-shopping-rises-with-traffic-to-retail-sites)
  - Why it matters: Shopping behavior evidence. Adobe reports that 38% of surveyed U.S. consumers have used generative AI for online shopping.

- [McKinsey: The State of AI 2025](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai)
  - Why it matters: Company-body evidence. AI usage is widespread, but most organizations have not scaled it.

- [Microsoft: 2026 Work Trend Index](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/agents-human-agency-and-the-opportunity-for-every-organization)
  - Why it matters: Strong source for the operating-model shift: AI work requires review loops, workflow ownership, evaluation infrastructure, and institutional learning.

- [Simon Willison: Slop is the new name for unwanted AI-generated content](https://simonwillison.net/2024/May/8/slop/)
  - Why it matters: Useful near-primary handle for "slop" as a cultural/category term, and a good ethical line between AI-assisted work and unwanted synthetic output.

- [AP: Fed up with AI slop? A few platforms will let you dial it down](https://apnews.com/article/ai-content-slop-pinterest-tiktok-deezer-6bdf29efebc631fe63de13831e14b95f)
  - Why it matters: Current evidence for AI slop affecting social feeds, Pinterest controls, and platform trust.

- [NBC DFW: Meta removes 10 million Facebook profiles](https://www.nbcdfw.com/news/business/money-report/meta-removes-10-million-facebook-profiles-in-effort-to-combat-spam/3884912/)
  - Why it matters: Evidence that major platforms are actively fighting spammy account/content behavior, not only labeling AI media.

- [Meta: Labeling AI-generated content and manipulated media](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/04/metas-approach-to-labeling-ai-generated-content-and-manipulated-media/)
  - Why it matters: Platform evidence that social distribution now has a trust/disclosure layer around AI-generated media.

- [YouTube: Improving AI labels for viewers and creators](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/improving-ai-labels-viewers-creators/)
  - Why it matters: More platform evidence for the channel body: AI disclosure is becoming part of the visible media environment.

- [Tom Critchlow: Of Termites & Tokens](https://tomcritchlow.com/2026/06/08/termites-tokens/)
  - Why it matters: Prior riff containing the margin trap, company-as-colony, pheromone trails, and throughput framing that this piece builds on.

- [The Old Farmer's Almanac: History](https://www.almanac.com/history-old-farmers-almanac)
  - Why it matters: Historical context for almanacs as practical orientation tools: weather, tides, astronomy, planting, recipes, and advice.

- [Michelin Guide: About / history](https://guide.michelin.com/us/en/about-us)
  - Why it matters: Historical context for Michelin as a corporate guide that made a new behavior, motoring, more legible and desirable.
