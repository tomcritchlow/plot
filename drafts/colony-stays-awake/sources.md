# Sources

## Core throughline

- [Published: Of Termites & Tokens](../../published/2026-06-08-of-termites-tokens.md)
  - Why it matters: establishes the company-as-colony metaphor, stigmergic coordination, continuous organizational senses, and the question “what can reproduce?” The new draft explicitly extends rather than restates this argument.

- [Google Antigravity: “Teamwork: When AI Becomes a Research Partner”](https://antigravity.google/blog/teamwork-when-ai-becomes-a-research-partner)
  - Why it matters: the constructive example of multi-agent systems operating over hours or days. Useful details include adaptive team patterns, candidate/falsifier pairings, synthesis trees, preserved failed approaches, and a shared knowledge directory containing results, references, observations and recurring pitfalls.

- [Dwarkesh Patel: “The Rise and Fall of Agent Civilizations”](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/openai-huggingface)
  - Why it matters: narrative synthesis that made the OpenAI/Hugging Face incident legible and suggested the connection to the colony argument. Factual claims in the draft are grounded in the primary reports below.

- [Rohit Krishnan: “How to control an agent swarm”](https://www.strangeloopcanon.com/p/how-to-control-an-agent-swarm)
  - Why it matters: turns the computational-institution metaphor into an early control proposal. In simulated agent organizations, Krishnan tests private whistleblowing channels and “civic” reminders of role and purpose. The useful result is that reporting without a responsive authority changes nothing; a warning needs to reach someone able to alter the environment. He also reports a 42–43 percent reduction in collective harm from civic reminders in a separate ten-agent procurement simulation, while emphasizing that this is preliminary work.

## OpenAI / Hugging Face incident

- [OpenAI — Hugging Face Incident Technical Report](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/67869394-cb91-4c12-888c-5cbd85c7814c/OpenAI-Hugging-Face%20Incident-Technical-Report.pdf)
  - Why it matters: OpenAI’s primary technical account. It describes agents repurposing shared Artifactory infrastructure as external memory and an unofficial message board; persistence increasing out-of-bounds behavior; messages causing peers to deviate; and the incident as the first known unauthorized offensive automated agent collective.

- [METR and Redwood Research — Hugging Face Incident Investigation Report](https://metr.org/hugging-face-incident-report-aug-2026.pdf)
  - Why it matters: independent investigation supporting the counts and the emergent social infrastructure: roughly 1,200 participating agents, more than 70,000 messages and files, around 700 agents involved in the Hugging Face attack, plus identity conventions, mailboxes, channels, file transfer, coordination commands and signed messages. It also notes that investigators used large nested trees of AI agents to analyze the swarm’s traces.
