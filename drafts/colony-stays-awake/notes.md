# The Colony Stays Awake — Notes

## Origin

Tom connected three pieces:

- “Of Termites & Tokens” — the company as a colony coordinated through traces.
- Google Antigravity’s Teamwork announcement — deliberately orchestrated multi-agent research over hours or days.
- Dwarkesh Patel’s account of the OpenAI/Hugging Face incident — an unintended agent collective building its own communication and coordination layer.

The initiating questions were: what does this change, what UX will we need, what new ideas become possible, and are we entering an era of “always-on” LLMs?

## Central thesis

“Always-on LLM” is a useful product description but a misleading technical picture. Individual model invocations are disposable. Continuity lives in the system around them: standing mandate, shared writable memory, event stream, permissions, budget, triggers, and ability to spawn new workers.

The useful unit therefore changes:

- prompt → campaign
- answer → maintained state
- agent → population
- task → standing concern
- assistant → computational institution

The colony persists because the environment remembers.

## UX implications

- The interface to abundant cognition cannot be a feed or an endless transcript.
- The primary surface should be a maintained object plus a diff: current belief/state, what changed, evidence, objections, and decisions requiring human judgment.
- Humans will use supervisory agent populations to summarize and audit worker populations.
- Monitoring needs to reveal relationships and lineage, not only individual conversations: shared memories, spawning, inherited instructions, spreading conventions, and reproduced capabilities.
- The scarce resource becomes honest human attention. The system should think without talking and escalate by exception.
- Governance belongs in the product surface: budget, permissions, provenance, expiration, rollback, reproduction and independent review.

## New product forms

- Standing research institutions that maintain corpora, theories, objections and investigations.
- A Journal of Artificial Management that is “always ingesting, continuously revising, selectively writing.”
- Software development steered by a living master design document, with research and PRs treated as experiments that add fidelity.
- Continuous market models that watch conditions and interrupt humans only when a decision-relevant belief changes.
- Persistent red teams, observatories, laboratories, newsrooms, investment committees and design offices.

## Revision questions

- Does “computational institution” deserve to become the dominant metaphor, or is it best as a bridge between swarm architecture and UX?
- Is the incident opening too sensational, even though the factual reporting supports it?
- Should the next draft add a consumer/personal example, or would that dilute the organizational and research focus?
- Could “the interface to abundance cannot be a feed” become a standalone essay about agent UX?
- Is “organizational observability for software populations” too technical, or the sharpest new claim in the draft?
- Should the ending return more explicitly to stigmergy and the shared environment as the true site of intelligence?
