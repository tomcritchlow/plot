## 3. Revision Request

Tom asked for four revisions:

1. Make the used-books point sharper and shorter.
2. Unpack the claim that better search can increase price dispersion and social welfare at the same time.
3. Push the piece to be more provocative and identify new lines of inquiry.
4. Bring in Frank Chimero's consumer-protection critique from `Beyond the Machine Extras`.

The revision made the counterintuitive price-dispersion hinge earlier and added the "Agents Or Protections?" section.

Important line that emerged:

This later softened into:

## 4. Source Constellation Expansion

Tom asked for more academic research and blog posts about consumer preferences and purchase decisions.

The strongest additions were:

New lines of inquiry:

- Preferences are staged, not stored.
- Agentic commerce turns non-consumption into a growth market.
- The fight moves from attention to consideration.
- Preference privacy may become more important than data privacy.

## 9. Layered Preferences

Tom then moved the title back:

He asked for references on preferences being in layers, from factual preferences like "I need it to be red" to values preferences like "I prefer independent stores."

The thread identified:

- Jonathan Gutman's means-end chain theory: attributes -> consequences -> values.
- Robert Woodruff's customer value hierarchy.
- Compensatory vs non-compensatory decisions as the basis for gates vs weights.

This became:

- `The Preference Stack`
- `Gates And Weights`

The key move:

> A preference is not one thing. It is a stack.
