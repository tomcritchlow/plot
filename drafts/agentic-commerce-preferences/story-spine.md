# Story spine: Models Of Desire

**Premise:** Agents can help construct useful preferences while changing who knows enough to bargain over them.

**Basis:** [Current manuscript](draft.md), [editorial checkpoint](notes.md), [source notes](sources.md). Updated 2026-09-14.

## Beats

### 1. Start with the obscure used book

- Cheap because nobody wants it—or because its ideal buyer cannot find it?
- [Ellison and Ellison](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24197): better matching in differentiated used books can coexist with higher prices and dispersion. This is not a rule for every market.

**Keep (draft):** “That is the counterintuitive hinge: better search can make markets more efficient and more expensive at the same time.”

### 2. Let the agent see below the search query

- A red object might mean visible, sentimental or gift-like; an independent store might represent convenience, trust or values.
- [Gutman's means-end chain](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/002224298204600207): attributes, consequences, values. The agent needs to know what the attribute does for the person.

### 3. Preferences are constructed, not simply retrieved

- [Bettman, Luce and Payne](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/25/3/187/1795625): context, effort and tradeoffs shape choice.
- The agent selects comparisons and asks questions that make one version of wanting actionable. It influences the preference it claims to serve.

### 4. Separate gates, weights and unresolved conflict

- A hard exclusion differs from a preference tradable against price. Surface language does not tell the agent which is which.
- [Tversky and Shafir](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1992.tb00047.x): conflict can defer choice. Agents may unlock trips, gifts and switches that never became transactions.

### 5. Better fit can alter the seller's pricing power

- [Aaron Kaye's recommendation research](https://www.aaron-kaye.com/research): better matching can weaken price competition.
- Distinguish that mechanism from revealing an individual's budget. The seller can serve a less price-sensitive audience without seeing each buyer's ceiling.

### 6. Ask what crosses the consumer–seller boundary

- The consumer agent may need rich preferences to compare privately; the seller may only need enough information to fulfil the request.
- Hypothetical grocery substitution: the agent knows what the family accepts; the seller need not know the premium the family would pay to avoid conflict.

**Keep (draft):** “An agent can know more about me while telling the market less.”

### 7. Move from convenience to preference rights

- Frank Chimero: “We don't need an agent, we need better legislation.” [Source](https://frankchimero.com/blog/2025/beyond-the-machine-extras/)
- A proposed preference wallet holds constraints, ambiguity and bargaining posture under user control. Portability, selective disclosure and the right to leave a choice unresolved matter.

## Landing

**Keep (draft):** “Does my agent tell the seller what would satisfy me—or what I could be persuaded to pay?”
