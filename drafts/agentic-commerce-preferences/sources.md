## Core Sources

- ["The Coasean Singularity? Demand, Supply, and Market Design with AI Agents"](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34468)
  - Market-design frame. AI agents reduce transaction costs and preference elicitation costs, expanding which market mechanisms become feasible.
- ["Match Quality, Search, and the Internet Market for Used Books"](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24197)
  - The key second-hand books example. Better search can increase match quality, consumer surplus, price dispersion, and prices in differentiated markets.
- [ProMarket explainer on used books and online competition](https://www.promarket.org/2018/04/26/used-books-can-tell-us-competition-online-markets/)
  - Accessible version of the used-books story and opening anecdote territory.
- [Gale and Shapley, "College Admissions and the Stability of Marriage"](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~billey/classes/562.winter.2018/articles/Gale.Shapley.pdf)
  - Deferred acceptance / stable matching background. Useful for the rank-order preference angle.
- [Aaron Kaye research page](https://www.aaron-kaye.com/research)
  - Personalized recommendations can improve match quality while weakening price competition.
- [Britannica on revealed preference theory](https://www.britannica.com/money/revealed-preference-theory)
  - Simple background for revealed preference.

## Consumer Preference Research

- [Bettman, Luce, and Payne, "Constructive Consumer Choice Processes"](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/25/3/187/1795625)
  - Preferences are often constructed in context rather than retrieved from a stable inner list.
- [Tversky and Shafir, "Choice under Conflict"](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1992.tb00047.x)
  - Conflict can lead to deferral, more search, or default choice. Useful for "deferred demand."
- [Jonathan Gutman, "A Means-End Chain Model Based on Consumer Categorization Processes"](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/002224298204600207)
  - Attributes -> consequences -> values. Best source for the layered preference stack.
- [Robert Woodruff, "Customer Value: The Next Source for Competitive Advantage"](https://www.scirp.org/reference/referencespapers?referenceid=2571649)
  - Customer value hierarchy. Similar layered model from attributes to consequences to goals.
- [Nielsen Norman Group, "Compensatory vs. Noncompensatory Decision Making"](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/compensatory-noncompensatory-decisions/)
  - Useful practitioner language for gates vs weights.

## Research Leads From The Thread

- [Simonson and Tversky, "Choice in Context: Tradeoff Contrast and Extremeness Aversion"](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/choice-context-tradeoff-contrast-extremeness-aversion)
  - Preferences are relational; the option set changes the choice.
- [Iyengar and Lepper, "When Choice is Demotivating"](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/when-choice-demotivating-can-one-desire-too-much-good-thing)
  - Classic choice overload source. Use lightly.
- [Novemsky, Dhar, Schwarz, and Simonson, "Preference Fluency in Choice"](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1509/jmkr.44.3.347)
  - Ease of forming a preference can shape the preference itself.
- [Ariely, Loewenstein, and Prelec, "Coherent Arbitrariness"](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/qjecon/v118y2003i1p73-106..html)
  - Willingness to pay can be arbitrary but coherent after anchoring.
- [Shocker, Ben-Akiva, Boccara, and Nedungadi on consideration sets](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02404071)
  - Purchase happens within a consideration set, not the entire universe of goods.
- [Philip Nelson, "Information and Consumer Behavior"](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Information-and-Consumer-Behavior-Nelson/e425402166b5f42aa82b0b034fd1389027181534)
  - Search / experience goods taxonomy.
- [Darby and Karni on credence goods](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc%3Aucp%3Ajlawec%3Av%3A16%3Ay%3A1973%3Ai%3A1%3Ap%3A67-88)
  - Credence goods and trust in markets.
