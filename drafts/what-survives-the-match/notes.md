## Origin

The draft began with a loose cluster of questions:

- Are we all going to work in a harness rather than a browser?
- What happens to customer acquisition when the computer performs the journey?
- Does Airbnb become an API call, and does its brand remain visible?
- Is the GEO debate optimizing a surface that no longer matters?
- Does richer personal context create more opportunity for brand matching, even as conventional attention disappears?
- Could customer service and the post-acquisition journey become the durable surface of a brand?

The user then pushed for more bite, more evidence and a framework that could tell brands where to invest and which marketplace brands are structurally exposed.

## Central thesis

The visible human journey contracts while the hidden machine journey explodes.

GEO is not useless, but a citation is an intermediate signal rather than a commercial outcome. The real contest is over the choice function: eligibility, weighting, selection, completion and future preference.

The marketplace version is sharper:

> The harness makes matching abundant. Marketplace value must begin where the match ends.

## Core distinctions

### Browser versus harness

- A browser is a place the customer goes.
- A harness is something that goes on the customer’s behalf.
- Instrumental journeys contract to intent, approval and outcome.
- Browsing for pleasure, taste, identity and exploration remains.

### Model speech versus agent action

- GEO measures what the model says.
- Commerce is what the agent does.
- Mentions and citations can affect eligibility or choice, but are not outcomes by themselves.

### Informational versus accountable brand

- Informational brand compresses research and comparison costs.
- Accountable brand names the institution responsible for the promise.
- Agents weaken the former more than the latter.
- Identity and desire remain separate brand functions that should not be flattened into trust alone.

## Brand investment framework

Current diagnostic: eligible → chosen → completed → more likely next time. The earlier P(value) equation was removed because these stages do not calculate business value.

Four layers:

1. **Callable / eligible:** machine-readable data, live state, policies, APIs, identity, permission.
2. **Preferable / chosen:** distinction, evidence, trust, fit, preference and scarce supply.
3. **Reliable / completed:** availability, fulfilment, payments, operations and quality control.
4. **Memorable / chosen again:** service recovery, guarantees, loyalty, permissioned memory and relationship.

Important editorial lines:

- Callability is the admission ticket. It is not a moat.
- Awareness matters when it changes the agent’s policy.
- Customer service becomes future customer acquisition.
- Every support interaction can become training data for the customer’s agent.

## Marketplace survival framework

Durable marketplace advantages:

- **Supply:** scarce, exclusive, fragmented or difficult-to-aggregate inventory.
- **State:** identity, reputation, history, loyalty and relationships accumulated across transactions.
- **Stakes:** payment, fulfilment, insurance, guarantees, disputes, recovery and responsibility for failure.

Two-axis map:

- Replaceable supply + little post-match work = wrapper; most exposed.
- Replaceable supply + substantial post-match work = operator; company can survive as plumbing while brand fades.
- Scarce supply + little post-match work = catalogue; useful but vulnerable to becoming a feed.
- Scarce supply + substantial post-match work = institution; strongest position.

Three fates:

1. Remain an institution.
2. Become plumbing.
3. Get reconstructed.

Critical nuance: a company can survive, and even grow, while its consumer brand disappears behind the harness.

## Company examples and intended use

- **Yelp:** name the reconstructible discovery/advertising layer, not predict company death. “The Yelp-shaped layer of Yelp.”
- **Angi:** strongest structural example of a business materially paid for consumer matches.
- **Tripadvisor / TheFork:** directional contrast between information/advertising and transaction infrastructure; explicitly avoid causal claim.
- **Zillow:** example of a discovery brand moving into touring, software, mortgages and closed-transaction economics.
- **DoorDash / Booking / OpenTable:** companies that may persist as post-match infrastructure even if the destination brand is less visible.
- **Airbnb:** the full-stack test. General harness can replace the demand interface, but supply, identity/reputation, payment, policy, support and AirCover are harder to reconstruct.

Do not turn this into stock analysis. The companies are examples of layers, and most contain more than one layer.

## Strong lines

- The phrase customer journey contains a hidden assumption: the customer is the one doing the travelling.
- The human journey has three steps: ask, approve, arrive. The machine journey might contain ten thousand.
- The customer journey is becoming private computation.
- A citation is evidence that your content was in the room, not that your brand won anything.
- GEO measures what the model says. Commerce is what the agent does.
- The harness eats the informational brand. It may strengthen the accountable brand.
- The harness makes matching abundant. Marketplace value must begin where the match ends.
- The interface disappears. The institution remains underneath it.
- Interfaces disappear on the happy path. Institutions appear on the unhappy path.
- The listing becomes an API response. AirCover is the brand.
- The brands in danger are the ones whose value ends at being found.
