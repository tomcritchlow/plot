## Generative visibility and agent choice

- [Aggarwal et al., “GEO: Generative Engine Optimization”](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09735v3)
  - Why it matters: establishes GEO as optimization for visibility inside generated answers, with impression metrics based on citation position, attributed word count and related factors.
  - Draft use: show that GEO’s native outcome is answer visibility, not transaction or downstream choice.
- [“Generative Engine Optimization: A Critical Survey,” 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14035)
  - Why it matters: reviews 45 studies and argues that the field lacks stable longitudinal, cross-platform evidence and downstream behavioural validation.
  - Caveat: preprint; use as a description of the present evidence gap, not a final verdict on GEO.
- [Shopping-agent behaviour study, arXiv:2508.02630](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02630)
  - Why it matters: reports model-specific position effects and differences in price/review sensitivity, supporting the claim that agent choice functions are not neutral.
- [Hunold, Kesler, Laitenberger and Schlütter, “Rankings of Online Travel Agents, Channel Pricing, and Consumer Protection”](https://www.zew.de/en/publications/rankings-of-online-travel-agents-channel-pricing-and-consumer-protection)
  - Why it matters: evidence that an existing travel intermediary’s ranking can discipline hotels that offer lower prices through other channels.

## Agentic commerce infrastructure

- [Universal Commerce Protocol](https://ucp.dev/)
  - Why it matters: open commerce protocol spanning discovery, checkout, identity, order management, returns and verticals such as lodging.
- [Google, “New AI tools and protocols for agentic commerce”](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/agentic-commerce-ai-tools-protocol-retailers-platforms/)
  - Why it matters: describes UCP, merchant-of-record positioning, branded Business Agents and Direct Offers inside agentic surfaces.
- [OpenAI, “Buy it in ChatGPT: Instant Checkout and the Agentic Commerce Protocol”](https://openai.com/index/buy-it-in-chatgpt/)
  - Why it matters: concrete implementation of agents ranking products and completing purchases while merchants remain merchant of record and handle fulfilment, returns and support.
- [Shahidi et al., “The Coasean Singularity? Demand, Supply, and Market Design with AI Agents”](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34468)
  - Why it matters: economic frame for agents reducing search, preference-elicitation and transaction costs, thereby changing feasible market design.

## Brand, trust and service

- [Erdem and Swait, “Brand Credibility, Brand Consideration, and Choice”](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/31/1/191/1812026)
  - Why it matters: supports the claim that credible brands increase consideration and choice, especially under uncertainty, with trustworthiness playing an important role.
- [Service-recovery research synthesis](https://lirias.kuleuven.be/retrieve/ec209dc9-411f-422e-93d9-ea5820bfadeb)
  - Why it matters: treats service recovery as a journey whose handling changes satisfaction, trust and broader evaluation of the firm.
- [Airbnb, “AirCover for guests”](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3218)
  - Why it matters: concrete example of post-match institutional value: rebooking and refund support when a host cancels or a stay has serious problems.
- [Airbnb 2020 S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1559720/000119312520294801/d81668ds1.htm)
  - Why it matters: historical evidence for Airbnb’s unusually strong direct/unpaid demand—91 percent of traffic in the first nine months of 2020.
  - Caveat: pandemic-era filing; retained as background and not used as a central claim in the current manuscript.

## Marketplace business models

- [Angi 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1705110/000170511026000011/angi-20251231.htm)
  - Why it matters: 57 percent of 2025 consolidated revenue came from fees paid by professionals for consumer matches. Clean evidence of material dependence on the matching layer.
- [Yelp 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1345016/000134501626000019/yelp-20251231.htm)
  - Why it matters: reports roughly $948 million in services advertising revenue and describes newer transaction/automotive initiatives such as RepairPal.
- [Tripadvisor 2025 results](https://ir.tripadvisor.com/static-files/aa7fa441-63ca-4330-b1cb-20d4a2498b8f)
  - Why it matters: Hotels & Other revenue declined 8 percent and media/advertising declined 12 percent while TheFork grew 22 percent.
  - Caveat: interesting structural contrast, not evidence that agents caused the performance divergence.
- [Zillow Group 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1617640/000161764026000015/z-20251231.htm)
  - Why it matters: shows Zillow expanding beyond discovery into touring infrastructure, agent software, mortgages and a performance fee tied to closed transactions.
- [Booking Holdings 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1075531/000107553126000009/bkng-20251231.htm)
  - Why it matters: merchant gross bookings rose to 70 percent of total gross bookings in 2025 from 63 percent in 2024, showing movement deeper into payment and transaction operations.
- [DoorDash 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1792789/000179278926000013/dash-20251231.htm)
  - Why it matters: describes Commerce Platform products including white-label fulfilment, ordering, branded apps, reservations and merchant support—post-match infrastructure that can persist behind another interface.
- [Etsy 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1370637/000137063726000019/etsy-20251231.htm)
  - Why it matters: possible later example of a marketplace combining differentiated supply with payment and transaction state.

- Whether preference policies will be portable between harnesses, and which party controls updates.
- Empirical work on brand choice when an AI agent makes the shortlist versus when the human browses directly.
