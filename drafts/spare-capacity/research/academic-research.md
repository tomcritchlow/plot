# Academic Research Angles

These are candidate academic handles for the riff. The goal is not to cite all of them, but to give the draft a few sharper conceptual pivots.

## 1. Flow Theory

Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi's flow theory gives a useful baseline: flow is not merely being busy or saturated. It tends to appear when goals are clear, feedback is immediate, and challenge and skill are balanced.

Use in the riff: poker multi-tabling creates flow because the goal, state, feedback, and decision grammar are unusually crisp. Agentic coding often lacks those properties: the feedback is delayed, quality is ambiguous, and the human may be supervising rather than acting.

Source: https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?research=7205

## 2. Attention Residue

Sophie Leroy's "attention residue" is probably the strongest cognitive-science fit. When people switch between unfinished tasks, part of the prior task remains cognitively active, making it harder to perform well on the next task.

Use in the riff: every agent you start creates a pending unfinished task. Multi-tabling LLMs is not free parallelism; it produces residue and return obligations.

Source: https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jobhdp/v109y2009i2p168-181.html

## 3. Task Switching Costs

Rubinstein, Meyer, and Evans on executive control and task switching can support the poker/AI distinction. Switching costs rise with rule complexity. Poker tables share the same rules and decision frame; LLM workstreams often do not.

Use in the riff: poker lets you switch tables without switching worlds. AI agents often ask you to switch problems, contexts, standards, and verification modes.

Source: https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/xhp274763.pdf

## 4. Slack Time and Innovation

Agrawal, Catalini, Goldfarb, and Luo's "Slack Time and Innovation" is unusually close to the piece. They distinguish high- and low-opportunity-cost time and show that slack increases both low-quality and high-quality projects, with effects on effort and coordination.

Use in the riff: slack is not pure good. It changes what kinds of projects get surfaced and pursued. This fits the draft's caution that unallocated time can become insight or drift.

Source: https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/orsc.2018.1215

## 5. Organizational Slack and the Inverted U

Nohria and Gulati argue that both too little and too much slack can hurt innovation. Too little slack prevents uncertain experimentation; too much slack can produce complacency and weak selection.

Use in the riff: the target is not idleness, but the right amount of uncommitted capacity. This helps avoid sounding like "do less work" as a universal prescription.

Source: https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/eurman/v15y1997i6p603-611.html

## 6. Exploration vs Exploitation

James March's exploration/exploitation frame gives the strategic version of the AI tradeoff. Exploitation improves known routines; exploration searches new possibilities. Adaptive systems tend to over-refine exploitation because it pays off faster.

Use in the riff: LLMs are excellent at making exploitation feel productive: more diffs, more summaries, more solved tickets. Walks and slack may preserve exploration capacity.

Source: https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ororsc/v2y1991i1p71-87.html

## 7. Real Options / Strategic Flexibility

Real options theory gives a finance bridge to the "option on future action" language. Flexibility has value under uncertainty because it lets firms defer, stage, expand, or abandon commitments as conditions change.

Use in the riff: reserve attention capacity is a personal real option. You are preserving the right, but not obligation, to act when the right opportunity becomes clearer.

Source: https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262693189/real-options-and-investment-under-uncertainty/

## 8. Cognitive Offloading

Risko and Gilbert's cognitive offloading work is relevant but should be used carefully. Offloading reduces cognitive demand, but changes the cognitive process rather than simply making the same work cheaper.

Use in the riff: AI is not just "typing for me." It changes where understanding, memory, and judgment live. The danger is offloading the very part of the task that creates edge.

Source: https://samgilbert.net/pubs/Risko2016TiCS.pdf

## 9. Automation Complacency and Bias

Parasuraman and Manzey's review argues that automation complacency appears under multiple-task load and that automation bias can cause omission and commission errors when decision aids are imperfect.

Use in the riff: when you are multi-tabling agents, you are exactly in a multiple-task monitoring environment. The risk is not just getting tired; it is trusting plausible outputs because attention is fragmented.

Source: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0018720810376055

## 10. Attention Restoration, Walking, and Incubation

Attention Restoration Theory and related work on walking and mind wandering support the walk as more than leisure. Nature can replenish directed attention; walking can increase creative ideation; undemanding tasks that allow mind wandering may help creative incubation.

Use in the riff: the walk is not anti-compute. It is a different cognitive mode: restoration, recombination, and incubation.

Sources:

- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19121124/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24749966/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22941876/

## Best Fits for the Draft

The strongest three to actually weave into the next revision:

1. Attention residue: every agent creates review debt and cognitive residue.
2. Slack time and innovation / organizational slack: slack is productive, but only at the right level.
3. Flow theory: the difference between flow as deep engagement and flow as attention saturation.

The best optional fourth is automation complacency, because it makes the "multi-tabling agents" risk sharper and more specific.

## 11. Clock Time, Task Time, and Work Discipline

E. P. Thompson's "Time, Work-Discipline, and Industrial Capitalism" is a strong historical frame for the whole riff. Thompson describes the transition from task-oriented work rhythms to industrial clock discipline: the shift from working according to necessity, season, and task, toward measuring labor as abstract time that must be used, disciplined, and accounted for.

Use in the riff: LLM multi-tabling can be framed as a new intensification of time-discipline. The old factory clock made idle time visible and morally suspect. AI agents make latency visible and operationally tempting. Every pause becomes a place where another task could be launched.

The most useful integration is not "AI is the factory clock," but subtler: AI revives a tension between task-time and clock-time. A walk, a thinking block, or an unallocated morning is task-oriented time: its value depends on what it lets you perceive, resolve, or become ready for. Multi-tabling agents turns that same interval into abstract throughput time.

Source: https://files.libcom.org/files/timeworkandindustrialcapitalism.pdf

## Poker-Specific Research

There does not seem to be a large academic literature specifically on "multi-tabling poker and attention/focus/burnout" as a narrow topic. But there are several adjacent poker studies that are useful.

### Online poker habits and multi-tabling

Ingo Fiedler's "The Gambling Habits of Online Poker Players" analyzes PokerStars account-level behavior across more than 2.1 million player identities. It explicitly measures multitabling and finds that, across the whole population, excessive multitabling was rare. This is useful because it separates the normal online poker population from the intense grinder/professional archetype.

Use in the riff: multi-tabling is not the average poker experience; it is a high-volume operating style. That makes it a better analogy for power users of LLM agents than for casual AI use.

Source: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1908161

### Multi-tabling as a differentiator among online poker groups

Barrault and Varescon's study comparing online and live regular poker players explicitly defines multitabling as playing several poker tables at once. In their online sample, multi-tabling significantly distinguished gambling-intensity groups, but not in the simple way one might assume: non-pathological online players were especially likely to report always multi-tabling.

Use in the riff: multi-tabling is not automatically pathological. It can be part of competent practice. The issue is not the number of tables by itself, but whether the player can preserve judgment, emotional regulation, and decision quality.

Source: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/299531060_Online_and_live_regular_poker_players_Do_they_differ_in_impulsive_sensation_seeking_and_gambling_practice

### High-earning online poker, stress, time, and workaholism

Hopley, Wagner, and Nicki studied high-earning online Texas Hold'em players and included multi-tabling as a demographic/practice variable. Their participants played between one and seventeen tables, and stress plus time spent playing were significant predictors of problem gambling. They also tested whether workaholism was a good frame for high-earning poker play and found it was not strongly related to problem gambling.

Use in the riff: professionalized poker sits awkwardly between game, work, addiction, and high-volume production. That is useful for the AI analogy: the problem is not simply "too much work" or "too much fun," but a strange high-control environment where volume, stress, and self-regulation interact.

Source: https://cdspress.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Anthony-A.B.-Hopley-Jennifer-Wagner-Richard-Nicki.pdf

### Sleep deprivation, tilt, and online poker

Hamel et al. tracked regular online poker players over 28 days. Sleep-deprived sessions showed higher emotional and behavioral tilt, more hands played, and worse financial results than at-rest sessions.

Use in the riff: the poker literature has a concrete mechanism for how volume and impaired self-regulation can create a loop: tired players play more hands, tilt more, and do worse.

Source: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2020.600092/full

### Tilt as loss of rational control

Moreau et al. created the Online Poker Tilt Scale, defining tilt as a deviation from a player's usual strategy at emotional, behavioral, and cognitive levels. Later work links tilt to loss of control and gambling disorder.

Use in the riff: "tilt" is a strong poker-native concept for what happens when attention and emotion break the decision system. In the AI piece, the analog might be not rage-clicking, but judgment drift: continuing to spawn, accept, or review agent work after the operator's edge has degraded.

Sources:

- https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/intgms/v17y2017i2p205-218.html
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32668576/

### Skill persists over volume, but volume is the measurement frame

Potter van Loon, van den Assem, and van Dolder use a huge online poker dataset to show that skill is persistent and becomes visible over enough hands. This does not speak to focus or burnout directly, but it explains why volume is so tempting in poker: the economic and statistical logic points toward more hands.

Use in the riff: online poker teaches a dangerous lesson that is partly true. More hands can reveal and monetize edge. The missing question is what volume does to the edge itself.

Source: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0115479
