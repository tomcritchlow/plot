# Sources

## Sean Goedecke, "Doing nothing at work"

https://www.seangoedecke.com/doing-nothing-at-work/

Why it matters: Sean gives the immediate trigger and the practical engineering version of the argument: high-impact work is time-dependent, and you need to be loose enough to notice and act. Useful phrase: "Nothing is a space things can happen in."

## Tom Critchlow, "The Capital of Ideas"

https://newmba.co/2023/11/01/strategic-finance/

Why it matters: Tom's earlier strategic finance piece gives the bridge from "good idea" to opportunity cost, finance, and opportunity capture. The useful connection is financial flexibility as a strategic advantage.

## Stephen V. Arbogast and Praveen Kumar, "Financial Flexibility and Opportunity Capture"

https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jacrfn/v30y2018i1p23-29.html

Why it matters: The paper's abstract argues that corporate finance and strategy should be coordinated, and that financial flexibility creates options on unique opportunities. Useful handle: "Reserve Financial Capacity" as an analogy for reserve attention capacity.

## Sophie Leroy, "Why is it so hard to do my work?"

https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jobhdp/v109y2009i2p168-181.html

Why it matters: Leroy's attention residue concept names the cognitive cost of switching away from unfinished work. This gives the draft a precise mechanism for why parallel agents create review debt and mental residue.

## Zvi Mowshowitz, "Slack"

https://thezvi.substack.com/p/slack

Why it matters: Zvi's definition of slack as "the absence of binding constraints on behavior" is a clean conceptual handle for why empty time matters. The post also connects slack to opportunities, long-term planning, and avoiding bad trades.

## Simon Willison, "Embracing the parallel coding agent lifestyle"

https://simonwillison.net/2025/Oct/5/parallel-coding-agents/

Why it matters: Simon is the best practical source for the pro-parallel-agent case. He is not naive about it: he names review as the bottleneck and frames parallel agents as useful for research, small maintenance, and carefully specified work.

## Gergely Orosz, "New trend: programming by kicking off parallel AI agents"

https://blog.pragmaticengineer.com/new-trend-programming-by-kicking-off-parallel-ai-agents/

Why it matters: Gergely turns the Simon pattern into a broader engineering trend and asks whether this breaks old assumptions about focus work. Useful counterpoint: Armin Ronacher's comment that the mind can only review so much.

## Hacker News, "Ask HN: How do you get into a flow state when using AI to code?"

https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48492118

Why it matters: Live practitioner texture around whether agentic coding destroys flow, replaces it with management, or creates a new kind of flow through selective multitasking. Useful as a chorus rather than a single authority.

## Rich Hickey, "Hammock Driven Development"

https://github.com/matthiasn/talk-transcripts/blob/master/Hickey_Rich/HammockDrivenDev.md

Why it matters: Older programming ancestor for stepping away from the machine. Hickey asks when you last thought about something for an hour, a day, a month, or a year, and argues that many serious software problems are problems of misconception rather than implementation.

## Ingo Fiedler, "The Gambling Habits of Online Poker Players"

https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1908161

Why it matters: Large account-level dataset from PokerStars that explicitly measures multitabling. Useful evidence that multitabling is a high-volume operating style rather than the default online poker experience.

## Barrault and Varescon, "Online and live regular poker players"

https://www.researchgate.net/publication/299531060_Online_and_live_regular_poker_players_Do_they_differ_in_impulsive_sensation_seeking_and_gambling_practice

Why it matters: Defines multitabling and compares poker practice across online/live and gambling-intensity groups. Useful because multitabling appears as a practice variable, not just a forum trope.

## Hopley, Wagner, and Nicki, "Problem gambling and workaholism in high earning online Texas Hold'em poker players"

https://cdspress.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Anthony-A.B.-Hopley-Jennifer-Wagner-Richard-Nicki.pdf

Why it matters: High-earning online poker study that includes multitabling, hours played, stress, problem gambling, and workaholism. Good bridge between poker-as-work and poker-as-risk.

## Hamel et al., "Sleep or Play Online Poker?"

https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2020.600092/full

Why it matters: Ecological study of online poker players showing that sleep-deprived sessions had more tilt, more hands played, and worse financial results. Useful for the loop between volume, fatigue, and decision quality.

## Moreau et al., "Online Poker Tilt Scale"

https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/intgms/v17y2017i2p205-218.html

Why it matters: Gives a validated way to think about tilt as emotional, behavioral, and cognitive deviation from normal play. Useful poker-native language for judgment drift.

## Potter van Loon, van den Assem, and van Dolder, "Beyond Chance?"

https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0115479

Why it matters: Shows persistent skill in online poker and explains why volume is so tempting: over enough hands, edge becomes measurable. The riff can ask what more hands do to the edge itself.

## E. P. Thompson, "Time, Work-Discipline, and Industrial Capitalism"

https://files.libcom.org/files/timeworkandindustrialcapitalism.pdf

Why it matters: Gives the deeper historical frame for clock time, task time, and the moralization of unused time. Useful for connecting AI multi-tabling to a longer history of making pauses visible, measurable, and suspect.
