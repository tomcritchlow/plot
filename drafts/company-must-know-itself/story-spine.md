# Story spine: On the Same Growth Wavelength

**Premise:** An annual report proposes a company that its readers can help build—or resist; the test is whether feedback can change the story.

**Basis:** [Current manuscript](draft.md), [editorial checkpoint](notes.md), [source notes](sources.md). Updated 2026-09-14.

## Beats

### 1. Enter through the strange object

- [Lowe's 1969 annual report](https://corporate.lowes.com/sites/lowes-corp/files/Annual%20reports%20and%20proxy%20statements/Lowes_AR_1969.pdf): psychedelic world map, computers, shipping routes and a retailer calling itself a cybernetwork.
- The design assigns suppliers, customers, staff and stockholders places inside one system. It is a theory of the company.

**Source quote — Lowe's:** “Feed-back and feed-forward are required for success.”

### 2. Put the Moon landing behind the report

- [James Webb](https://archive.org/details/spaceagemanageme0000webb) treats large-scale organization as Apollo's transferable achievement.
- [Martin Parker](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1744935909337759): “organization produces excess, and excess requires organization.” Extraordinary achievement rests on mundane coordination.

### 3. Ask where the network's intelligence lives

- [ARPANET's first message](https://www.lk.cs.ucla.edu/internet_first_words.html), LO, gives 1969 another coordination image.
- [Sam Schillace's circuit/packet distinction](https://sundaylettersfromsam.substack.com/p/the-network-always-beats-the-castle): fixed reporting paths versus information moving through intelligent edges.
- Lowe's straddles both. Preserve the analogy's limits: Apollo also distributed expertise; ARPANET also required institutions.

### 4. Show the future being made tangible

- The report's 58 stores become 1,142 missing stores against its 1,200-store ambition. Store dimensions and sites give the imagined future physical form.
- Feed-forward: readers are invited to act toward that future. Its intended effect is visible; its actual effect still needs evidence.

### 5. Show the company listening

- The poorly selling door needs sales data, customer explanation and buyers' sourcing knowledge.
- Grand-opening wish lists, store meetings and stockholder questionnaires turn market responses into possible inputs. The market becomes one of the company's senses.

### 6. The narrative participates in the economy

- [Shiller](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23075): stories motivate economic action. [Soros](https://www.georgesoros.com/2012/06/02/remarks_at_the_festival_of_economics_trento_italy/): participants' interpretations and actions affect one another.
- The annual report gathers local activity into a public operating model. The [2025 report](https://corporate.lowes.com/sites/lowes-corp/files/2026-04/lowes-2025-annual-report.pdf) provides a brief same-company echo, not a second essay.

### 7. Alignment can conceal an echo

- Employee ownership, commissions and bonuses give agreement incentives. A growth story may select and reward its own believers.
- Separate management's self-description from demonstrated adoption. A coherent narrative needs channels capable of challenging it.

**Keep (draft):** “Corporate fantasy is what happens when feed-forward overwhelms feedback.”

### 8. End on the reply card

- The detachable questionnaire asks for criticism while inviting new shareholders.
- It adds the reader to the system. The blank lines matter only if an unwelcome answer can alter something.

## Landing

**Keep (draft):** “The harder question was what Lowe's would do with a reply it did not want.”

## Open

- Verify retained historical details and quotes against report pages before publication.
- Find an actual response or changed decision; do not infer success from the report itself.
- Keep the reply card central; shorten packet switching if it delays the payoff.
