# Story spine: Things I Think About AI

**Premise:** Removing today's constraints on speed, memory, interfaces and coordination will change which products and institutions are possible.

**Basis:** [Current manuscript](draft.md), [editorial checkpoint](notes.md), [source notes](sources.md). Updated 2026-09-14. Preserve the twelve-item form and its August 2026 viewpoint; these are convictions and predictions.

## Beats

### 1. Broadband AI changes the product

- Latency shapes what interaction can be. Faster tokens permit responsive voice, branching and interfaces.
- [Google's diffusion work](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/diffusion-gemma-faster-text-generation/) is the concrete anchor; Google winning remains a wager.

**Keep (draft):** “I am waiting for the broadband era of AI.”

### 2. Memory becomes a maintained process

- Disagree with [Noah Brier's memory pessimism](https://newsletter.brxnd.ai/p/things-i-think-i-think-about-ai-2026).
- Compact useful context; revise values, resolve contradictions and forget. Link the fuller [memory essay](../memory-layers/story-spine.md).

### 3. The answer acquires a form

- Maps for geography, manipulable comparisons for buying, navigable systems for code.
- Generated interfaces become appropriate to the task; Codex's lead is a personal assessment to revisit.

### 4. Analytics loses the journey before it loses the sale

- Delegated research hides rejected alternatives and intent behind observable transactions.
- Identity can help but creates privacy questions; use outcomes and experiments as well as traces.

### 5. Content rights fragment capability

- Bilateral licensing and crawler access can make identical tasks work differently across products.
- NYT/model pairings are hypothetical examples, not claims about current agreements.

### 6. Consumer use cases are an opening

- Contrast capable code assistance with unreliable local recommendations, maps and shopping.
- Google has relevant assets; current product comparisons need dated examples before publication.

### 7. Good writing tools coexist with abundant bad writing

- [Tom's writing-quality observation](https://x.com/tomcritchlow/status/2083260043915317673): models can help structure, edit and generate alternatives.
- Choosing what deserves to be said remains a different problem.

### 8. A film escapes the AI discourse

- Prediction: a feature-length AI-produced film finds mainstream success by early 2027 for its artistic appeal.
- Define “AI-produced” and “mainstream success”; lower production cost expands who can attempt the work.

### 9. Integration costs change the set of possible acquisitions

- Agents may help translate schemas, connect systems and recover knowledge.
- Culture, ownership and authority remain constraints. Lower coordination costs can favor small firms or conglomerates; neither outcome follows automatically.

### 10. Machine readers change the shape of code

- Local explicitness, smaller modules and possible duplication; tie to [taste versus elegance](../../captures/2026/08/2026-08-19-095818-taste-versus-elegance.md).
- Test whether invariants remain consistent across copies. Easy generation alone does not make duplication good design.

### 11. Unknown callers must earn an interruption

- Screening combines identity and reason for calling.
- Preserve legitimate unfamiliar callers; predict a new front door, not universal refusal.

### 12. Make the weird consequences observable

- Move from faster existing work to new forms of work and institutions.
- Revisit changed beliefs, integration timelines and film audiences—not just tokens, pilots and demos.
