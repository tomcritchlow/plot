# Notes

## Origin

Prompted by Noah Brier's “Things I Think I Think About AI (2026 Edition).” The goal is not to rebut Noah point-by-point, but to use his list as permission for a wide-angle set of convictions and predictions.

## Original outline

1. Tokens per second is underappreciated. We are still waiting for AI like dial-up users waited for broadband. Speed enables different products, not merely faster ones. Google's work on diffusion models and custom chips may position it well.
2. Disagree with Noah's pessimism on memory. A person's useful context is compact; the opportunity is a memory harness that summarizes, compacts, extracts, remembers and forgets. It eventually has to model values. AI needs an overnight memory process.
3. Interfaces need invention. Codex is the best GUI but remains text-oriented. AI will develop native maps, shopping, visual, interactive and problem-specific surfaces.
4. Web analytics is already dead. Either measurement dissolves or agents need standardized session identity and provenance.
5. Publisher licensing and fair use will explode as agents require live content. Bilateral deals will make model capabilities fragment in ways that are nonsensical to users. A healthy licensing ecosystem feels unlikely.
6. Major models remain surprisingly poor at maps, shopping and local business information. Consumer AI innovation is lagging coding and enterprise workflows. This is Google's game to lose.
7. Models are getting good at writing. Writers will adopt them as they adopted Word, spellcheck and Google Docs, even while slop proliferates.
8. A feature-length AI-generated movie will achieve mainstream success in early 2027 because it is good, not because it is AI. Lowering the cost of production expands who can make ambitious work.
9. AI will shorten business-integration timelines and change M&A economics. The one-person billion-dollar company and the trillion-dollar conglomerate are two consequences of falling coordination costs.
10. Code changes shape when AI writes and reads nearly all of it: more duplication, smaller modules, new repository structures and richer machine-facing context beyond AGENTS.md.
11. Unverified calls become unanswerable.
12. The truly weird second-order consequences have barely begun.

## Underlying structure

The list currently moves through four nested scales:

- Model substrate: speed and memory.
- Product surface: interfaces and consumer capability.
- Information economy: analytics, licensing, writing and video.
- Institutional structure: M&A, code, identity and general weirdness.

## Revision questions

- Does “fully AI-generated movie” need a more precise definition, or is the coming definitional argument part of the point?
- Should the web analytics item propose a concrete protocol, or remain a provocation?
- The M&A point is one of the more novel claims; it could support a separate essay.
- The code-shape point connects directly to the taste-versus-elegance capture and could also become its own seed.
- The ending could become stranger and more specific rather than simply widening the aperture.
